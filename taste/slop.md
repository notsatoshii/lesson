# Slop: the tells

Each item below, present in the plan without a reason written in the brief, is an automatic finding: severity 2 in try and apply segments and in anything the learner sees or hears, severity 3 elsewhere. Three or more on one plan cap the engagement score at 5.

## Structure

- Lecture, then quiz. The whole plan is one learn segment followed by one check.
- An agenda slide as the opener. The hook is a list of what the hook should have been.
- "Any questions?" as the check segment.
- An icebreaker with no connection to the content.
- The same activity shape three times (three discussions, three worksheets, three "share with a partner").
- A talk of twenty minutes or more before anyone does anything.
- A "reflection" paragraph as the only assignment.
- Homework that is more of the worksheet, with no own-material step.
- Segments all the same length regardless of beat.
- A "break" that is the only place the active ratio is met.
- A hook question that is never answered later in the lesson.
- Questions answered only by hands up or a volunteer while everyone else watches.
- An explanation that never says the common wrong belief out loud, when the topic has one.

## Text

- Bullet walls on a slide, read aloud.
- Learning objectives in teacher jargon: "learners will be able to demonstrate an understanding of". The outcome says what they can do and what they made.
- Acronyms used before they are defined, or never defined.
- A glossary nobody is sent to.
- Korean that reads as a translation: 당신, noun stacks, passive strings, idioms carried over, 보장 / 반드시 promises.
- "Fun" announced in the title ("Fun with fractions!"). If it is fun the title does not need to say so.
- Instructions that tell the learner what they will feel ("you'll love this part").
- The same sentence as the slide headline, the spoken line and the handout.

## Gamification

- Points for attendance.
- Badges for trivial things: opening the app, finishing the intro, a first login.
- A leaderboard of individuals in a classroom.
- A timer on a question that needs judgement.
- A mascot with a catchphrase.
- "Level up!" with nothing unlocked.
- XP for self-reported time or self-rated confidence.
- A quiz-show format where one person answers and thirty watch.
- A streak that punishes a missed day in a course that meets weekly.
- A reward that is a picture of a trophy.
- An expected, tangible reward for doing or finishing: "finish and you get a sticker", "complete the module, get a voucher".
- A leaderboard or ranking with no supportive individual feedback, or with nothing said about what the learner at the bottom sees.
- Mandatory badges plus a public leaderboard.
- A streak that resets to zero and says "you lost your streak".
- "Play a Kahoot" (or any quiz tool) with no mode named: team or individual, speed or accuracy, named or anonymous.
- Mechanics aimed at Bartle player types ("a duel for the Killers").
- A choice that pays points ("pick your path, earn 10 XP").
- A game layer in a multi-week course that never changes after week 1.

## Humour

- A pun on the lesson title.
- The meme from two years ago.
- The joke that explains itself afterwards.
- An emoji as the punchline.
- Humour at a learner's expense, including "the one who always".
- A comedy intro with no connection to the content.
- "Fellow kids" slang in a plan for band C or D.
- A joke in a check segment, a safety step or a consent moment.
- A laugh pause written into the script.
- A warm-up or mood-change joke unrelated to the content (the 분위기 전환 line before the lesson starts).
- A narrator who says more than once that they are bad at the thing they are teaching.
- A sarcastic narrator in band A or B.
- 아재개그 in band C or older, or a pun that is not on the content word.

## Story

- A mascot that only announces segments: it never reacts, never needs help, and could be deleted.
- A frame that is only a setting: no disruption ("but one day") and no resolution ("until finally").
- The story as a wrapper, with the concept in a bullet list beside it.
- A scenario whose only feedback is "correct" or "wrong".
- An anecdote or image that is interesting but only near the concept.

## Visuals

- Clip-art people.
- Stock photos of handshakes, laptops and coffee, people pointing at a screen.
- A diagram of two boxes and an arrow.
- Rainbow highlights on a screenshot; more than three callouts on one screenshot.
- A screenshot with no callouts, or with the whole screen boxed.
- A video that is a recorded lecture.
- Three typefaces on one slide deck.
- The template theme with the swoosh.
- An icon set mixed from three families.
- A chart with no title that states its finding.

## Practical

- A toy problem when a real one was available ("add two numbers" in a lesson for accountants).
- "Imagine a company that..." when the learners work at one.
- An assignment nobody would do unassigned.
- An artifact that is never used again, in the course or in the learner's life.
- Practice on the teacher's example only; the learner's own material never appears.
- A tool used in the lesson that the learner will not have afterwards.

## Assessment

- A multiple-choice quiz as the only evidence.
- Self-rating of confidence counted as evidence.
- "Did everyone get that?" as the check.
- A check that tests recall of the slide, not use of the skill.
- Evidence the teacher or app never looks at.
- A pass mark with no retry path.

## Not slop (do not flag)

- A talk-shaped plan when the mode is talk and the brief says why the room cannot do.
- A quiz when the brief needs certification evidence and says so; it still cannot be the only evidence.
- An icebreaker when the brief says the group must meet each other first, and it is tied to the content.
- A timer in a try segment when speed is the skill being taught.
- A leaderboard that is opt-in, for band D or above, when the brief asks for it.
- Repetition in band A: it is the mechanic, not a tell.
- A plain plan when the brief's register is plain. Restraint is a choice when the brief makes it.
- One low-bar "you tried" badge on the first try segment only, for B–C, when the brief says the learners are low in confidence; every later badge is tied to evidence.
- A one-off lesson whose game layer never changes: novelty is on its side.
- One narrator line about their own slip with a tool.
- A cap or ratio missed by a little with no other harm: a house-default breach, severity 2 at most in the critic, never a slop tell on its own.

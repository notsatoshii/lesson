# Age bands

Every critic judges against the band in the brief, not against their own age. When a class spans two bands, plan for the younger one's attention and the older one's dignity.

**The caps and ratios are house defaults, not research claims.** The minute caps, segment and session lengths, words per screen and active-to-passive ratios below are planning conventions; the house rules or the brief may override them. There is no research basis for a fixed attention span in minutes: attention lapses are short and frequent, and a change of activity is what resets it. So a breach on the number alone is severity 2 at most. Severity 1 needs a concrete harm on top of the number: the plan cannot run in its slot, a learner loses dignity, or something is unsafe. The humour, reward and competition rules marked with a source at the end of this file are evidence-backed and are judged at full severity.

## Rules across bands

- **Doing beats listening at every age.** The ratio of learner-active minutes (try, apply, check) to passive minutes is at least 1:1 in band E and above, 2:1 in A–C. These ratios are house defaults: missing one on the number alone is severity 2 at most.
- **Passive stretches end in an activity change.** The cap says roughly how long; the fix is a change of activity (a question everyone answers, a prediction, a demonstration), not a shorter monologue.
- **Rewards for children are unexpected or informational.** In A–B a reward appears when something happens in the lesson (the character's problem is solved, three steps are done and named) or as a surprise. It is never promised in advance for doing or finishing the task; expected tangible rewards hurt children's interest more than adults'.
- **Any ranking is paired with supportive feedback.** Every leaderboard, ranking or team table, even an opt-in one, comes with individual supportive feedback on the learner's own progress, and the plan says what the learner or team at the bottom sees.
- **Compete as teams, get feedback as an individual.** At every age, where there is a contest the unit of competition is a team and the unit of feedback is the individual. Individual head-to-heads are opt-in, against a benchmark or the learner's own earlier result.
- **Several small choices for children.** A–C get more than one small choice per lesson (which example, which order, which colour); a choice followed by a reward loses its effect.
- **The first wrong answer is private in A–C.** Own screen, own whiteboard, a pair; never hands up or named. In B a character is wrong first.
- **Band B: deadpan yes, sarcasm no.** A deadpan adult who is wrong lands; a sarcastic narrator does not. Children understand sarcasm by about 8 but rarely find it funny, and at 9 to 10 they rate it as mean and identify with its target. Dry irony is safe from C up.
- **G: mechanics are checked with learners.** A game mechanic for band G is chosen with learners of this age, or the plan says how it will be checked with them in the first session and what replaces it if they reject it.
- **Humour never costs a learner dignity.** A joke that a shy learner in the room could feel is about them is severity 1. A joke about the tool, the narrator, the absurdity of the task, or a character is fine.
- **Humour density:** one beat per segment at most; none in check segments; none in safety or consent moments. One related beat just before the first try segment is welcome.
- **Story stakes match the band:** A–B a character's problem; C–D a scenario they could be in next year; E–G their own work or life, this week. In A–B the character reacts to the learner, stays the same across segments, and has a problem the learner helps solve.
- **Reading level is checked on the learner-facing text only,** not on teacher notes.
- **Game mechanics must survive the "why do I care" test** for the band: a sticker works at 6 because it is a sticker; at 35 it works only if it stands for evidence of something real.
- **Mixed-age rooms** (families, community classes): plan every try segment so a pair of different ages can do it together with distinct roles.

## Sources for the evidence-backed rules

- No fixed attention span; activity change resets attention: [Wilson & Korn via Faculty Focus](https://www.facultyfocus.com/articles/instructional-design/student-attention-spans); [Bunce, Flens & Neiles 2010](https://wikicap.ulb.be/images/5/57/Bunce_Flens_et_al_2010.pdf).
- Expected tangible rewards undermine interest, more for children; competence feedback raises it: [Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf); [Deci, Koestner & Ryan 2001](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2001_DeciKoestnerRyan.pdf).
- Rankings need supportive feedback: [AEA Papers & Proceedings 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/aea/apandp/v113y2023p508-13.html); mandatory badges plus a public leaderboard backfired: [Hanus & Fox 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2014.08.019).
- Cooperation beats competition; competition works best combined with collaboration: [Hilk 2013](https://conservancy.umn.edu/items/5e0a2b70-7901-443a-ac5e-2f787065e056); [Sailer & Homner 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w).
- Choice effects are larger for children and for several small choices: [Patall, Cooper & Robinson 2008](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/2008_PatallCooperRobinson_PsychBulletin.pdf).
- Sarcasm and irony by age: [BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/what-age-do-children-recognise-difference-between-sarcasm-and-irony).
- Responsive, consistent characters for young children: [Calvert, Georgetown](https://college.georgetown.edu/news-story/technology-for-tots/).
- Older adults: engagement and adherence over performance, intrinsic motivators, co-design: [Hong & Choi](https://journal.gerontechnology.org/currentIssueContent.aspx?aid=4979); [Koivisto & Malik](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8437506).
- Streak breaks demotivate; flexible streaks and repairs help: [Silverman & Barasch via Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2024-03-streaks.html).

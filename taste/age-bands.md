# Age bands

Every critic judges against the band in the brief, not against their own age. Numbers are working defaults for planning, not research claims; the house rules or the brief may override them. When a class spans two bands, plan for the younger one's attention and the older one's dignity.

| band | ages | longest passive stretch | segment length | session length | reading | humour that lands | humour that fails | game mechanics that work | mechanics that backfire |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** young children | 5–7 | 3 min | 5–8 min | 30–40 min | pictures first, ≤ 1 short sentence per screen, read aloud | silly, physical, repetition, a character who gets it wrong | irony, sarcasm, wordplay | stickers, collecting, "help the character", movement, songs | timers, leaderboards, losing points |
| **B** children | 8–11 | 5 min | 8–12 min | 40–50 min | short paragraphs, ≤ 40 words per screen, concrete nouns | absurd scenarios, deadpan adults, running gags, gross-but-safe | sarcasm aimed at them, adult references, puns on jargon | quests, badges, team points, mystery to solve, levels | public ranking of individuals, speed pressure on thinking |
| **C** early teens | 12–14 | 7 min | 10–15 min | 45–60 min | plain prose, define every term once, examples from their world | dry understatement, self-aware narrator, memes only if the brief allows and they are current | trying too hard, "fellow kids" slang, anything that could embarrass one student | choice, duels vs a benchmark, unlocking, streaks, small-team challenges | forced solo performance, cutesy mascots, anything that feels like a worksheet with points |
| **D** teens | 15–18 | 10 min | 12–20 min | 50–90 min | adult reading level, real sources, no padding | dry, ironic, honest about the boring parts, callbacks | mascots, cheer, humour that pretends the material is easier than it is | real stakes (portfolio, a thing they use), leaderboards opt-in, boss challenges, mastery levels | points for attendance, badges for trivial things, childish art |
| **E** adults, learning for work | 19–60 | 12 min | 10–20 min | 60–120 min (with a break every 50) | plain language, ELI5 analogy for each new concept, jargon only after defined, phone-readable | dry, understated, about the work and the tools, never about the learner; one line, no laugh pause | puns, forced fun, icebreakers unrelated to content, anything that costs dignity | own-work artifacts, XP for evidence, streaks, duels vs a "before", checklists, small-group reveals | public rankings, speed timers on judgement, childish rewards, "energisers" |
| **F** adults, personal interest | any adult | 15 min | 10–25 min | 60–120 min | as E, with more room for depth | as E, warmer, story-led | as E | collections, mastery paths, show-and-tell, clubs | competition unless chosen |
| **G** seniors | 65+ | 10 min | 10–15 min | 45–90 min | larger type, one idea per screen, no acronyms, step numbers always visible | dry, warm, about shared experience; never about age or technology fear | anything fast, anything that implies they are slow | collections, progress shown as a path, pairs, repeat-at-home sheets | timers, streaks with penalties, leaderboards |

## Rules across bands

- **Doing beats listening at every age.** The ratio of learner-active minutes (try, apply, check) to passive minutes is at least 1:1 in band E and above, 2:1 in A–C.
- **Humour never costs a learner dignity.** A joke that a shy learner in the room could feel is about them is severity 1. A joke about the tool, the narrator, the absurdity of the task, or a character is fine.
- **Humour density:** one beat per segment at most; none in check segments; none in safety or consent moments.
- **Story stakes match the band:** A–B a character's problem; C–D a scenario they could be in next year; E–G their own work or life, this week.
- **Reading level is checked on the learner-facing text only,** not on teacher notes.
- **Game mechanics must survive the "why do I care" test** for the band: a sticker works at 6 because it is a sticker; at 35 it works only if it stands for evidence of something real.
- **Mixed-age rooms** (families, community classes): plan every try segment so a pair of different ages can do it together with distinct roles.

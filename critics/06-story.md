# 06 Story and humour

Lens: does the lesson have a narrative spine and a voice, with dry humour that fits the age band, and does the concept live in what happens in the story rather than beside it? Runs from cycle 2 whenever the gate passes (a plan that does not fit its slot has no room for a story, and the gate is what says whether it fits).

## Checks

- **A frame exists:** the Story frame block names a setting, a character and stakes, and the hook (S1) opens it. "The lesson is about spreadsheets" is a topic, not a frame. Stakes are shown in a situation, never announced ("this matters because").
- **Story-spine completeness:** write the plan's frame as a story spine: "Once upon a time… Every day… But one day… Because of that… Until finally… And ever since then…". If "But one day" (the disruption) and "Until finally" (the resolution) cannot be filled from the plan, the frame is a setting, not a story (severity 2). Where the hook is a Meyer-style first act (a picture or situation with almost no words), the resolution is the third act: the real answer shown against the learner's own guess.
- **The concept lives in the events:** the concept is carried by what happens in the story (what happened, to whom, with what consequence), not by a bullet list beside a story wrapper. Only the first gives the memory advantage stories have over plain exposition. A beat that is interesting but only near the concept is the seductive-details case: a finding even when it is good writing. For band E learners with high prior knowledge, accept a thinner frame; experts gain less from narrative.
- **Stakes match the band:** `taste/age-bands.md`, "story stakes match the band". A–B a character's problem; C–D a scenario they could be in next year; E–G their own work or life, this week. A band E plan whose stakes belong to a cartoon fox is a finding.
- **A–B character reacts, stays, needs help:** in bands A–B a guide character (a) reacts to what the learner just did, (b) is the same character, with the same name and manner, across every segment, and (c) has a problem the learner helps solve. A mascot that only announces segments has none of the three: a finding (severity 2).
- **Scenarios show consequences before verdicts:** in a scenario or decision segment (band E especially), each decision shows what happens next in the story before, or instead of, "correct" or "wrong". The wrong options are things a real colleague would plausibly do. A scenario whose only feedback is right or wrong is a quiz in costume (severity 2). Decisions come before the content they need, not after a block of exposition.
- **The story returns:** at least two callbacks from the frame appear in later segments, and the reward segment closes a thread the hook opened. A frame that appears in S1 and never again is a prologue, not a spine.
- **One voice:** the narrator or guide sounds like one person across every learner-facing line: instructions, transitions, captions, app copy, error messages. Quote two lines that do not sound like the same person.
- **Register is dry:** deadpan, understatement, the honest aside about the boring part, absurd-but-plausible scenarios. Cheer, exclamation marks and "fun!" announcing itself are findings.
- **Every joke classified:** label each humour beat related (about the concept or the task), unrelated, self-disparaging (the narrator) or other-disparaging (anyone else). Related: keep. Unrelated, including a warm-up or mood-change joke before the content starts: a finding (severity 2 where the learner sees or hears it); it adds attention and no learning. Other-disparaging: severity 1. Self-disparaging: see the cap below.
- **Resolvable unaided:** a learner of this band can see the incongruity and resolve it without anyone explaining. If the plan has to explain the joke, or the joke needs knowledge the band lacks, cut it.
- **Narrator self-deprecation capped:** at most one self-deprecating beat per lesson, about a tool or task slip ("I pasted it into the wrong window"), never about the narrator's competence in the subject being taught. A second one, or one that says the narrator is bad at the subject, is a finding (severity 2).
- **Density and placement:** one humour beat per segment at most; none in check segments; none in safety or consent moments. Count the beats in the story / humour column and the segment detail, not the frame's promise. Look for, and allow, one related beat immediately before the first try segment, where it lowers the cost of a wrong first move.
- **Never at a learner's expense (severity 1):** a joke a shy learner could feel is about them, about a mistake they are likely to make, or about their group (age, job, level) is severity 1. Jokes about the tool, the narrator's small tool slip, the task's absurdity or a character are fine.
- **Never about a real named person.** No colleagues, bosses, public figures, competitors.
- **Band bans:** no puns on jargon for C and up; no "fellow kids" slang anywhere; no irony or sarcasm for A; in B a deadpan adult who is wrong is fine, a sarcastic narrator is not (children understand sarcasm by about 8 but at 9 to 10 rate it as mean and side with its target); no mascots or cheer for D–E; nothing about age or technology fear for G. 아재개그-style puns only in A–B, and only on the content word; from C up they are mere wordplay. Quote the band row you applied.
- **Silent read:** the joke works when read silently on a phone, with no laugh pause, no delivery and no voice. A line that needs a comedian is a finding in online, guide and video modes.
- **Native, not translated:** jokes are written in the house-rule language from the start. A Korean line that reads as translated English humour (English rhythm, a setup–punchline shape Korean does not use, a reference the band would not know) is a finding; the fix is a new line, not a better translation. In a 존댓말 plan the line must stay funny in 존댓말; one that only works in 반말 rhythm fails.
- **Costs no minutes:** story and humour live inside a segment's existing minutes. A beat that needs two minutes of its own, in a segment with none spare, is a finding here and for 01.
- **The vanish test:** remove the character and the frame. If the activity still makes sense but something is lost, the story is right. If nothing is lost, the story is decoration (severity 2 or 3). If the activity stops making sense, the story is load-bearing and must be stated plainly in the instruction, not only in the story column (severity 1).
- **Every beat carries a concept:** for each story beat and joke, name the concept or practice it sets up, illustrates or rewards. One that carries none is a finding (SKILL.md: fun serves the learning).

## Humour tells that fail

- the pun on the lesson title
- the forced mascot catchphrase
- the meme that was current two years ago
- the joke that explains itself in the next sentence
- emoji as the punchline
- the "we know this part is boring, haha" that then stays boring
- the joke in brackets after a serious instruction
- the same joke twice with the noun changed
- the warm-up joke with no link to the content (the 분위기 전환 line)
- the narrator who keeps saying they are bad at the subject
- the 아재개그 in a band C or older plan, or one that is not on the content word

## Mode notes

- **live:** the teacher delivers the line, so timing may help, but the plan still writes the line out. Watch for jokes that need the room to react; they die in a quiet class.
- **online:** every line is read silently on a phone. No laugh pause; no aside that depends on tone.
- **video:** the storyboard carries the beat (on screen, caption or voice). A joke only in the voice track fails for viewers reading captions. Jokes about the topic help recall in recorded lectures; one related beat per segment, no unrelated warm-up jokes.
- **guide:** humour is rare and lives in asides, never inside a numbered step. The frame may be a one-line scenario at the top.
- **talk:** the frame may be the whole arc; callbacks matter more here than anywhere, and the close must land one.

## Anchors

Examples of each level, and a good and a bad line per main check: `critics/examples/06-story.md` (sections `## 4`, `## 7`, `## 9`, `## Pairs`). Compare the plan with those segments before placing it.

- **4** (see examples `## 4`): the plan has no story frame, or a frame that appears once in S1 and never returns, or a story wrapper with the concept in a list beside it; humour is cheer, puns on the title, an unrelated warm-up joke, or a joke a learner could feel is about them; or the voice changes between the instructions and the app copy; or a joke sits in the check segment or the consent moment; or a scenario answers every decision with "correct".
- **7** (see examples `## 7`): the plan has a frame with setting, character and stakes of the band's kind, opened in the hook and returned to at least once, and the spine's disruption and resolution can be filled; the concept is mostly carried in the events; one voice holds across most text; every joke is related, dry and under the density cap; one or two beats are decoration (they vanish without loss), or one line reads as translated, or a scenario decision shows a verdict before its consequence; nothing costs a learner dignity.
- **9** (see examples `## 9`): the plan has a frame whose full spine can be written from the plan, returns at least twice and closes in the reward against the learner's own guess; the concept happens in the story's events; in A–B the character reacts, stays the same and needs the learner's help; every scenario decision shows its consequence; every joke is related, resolvable by the band unaided and names the concept it carries, with one related beat placed just before the first try; at most one narrator slip, about a tool; one voice, native to the house-rule language, that a reader could pick out of a line-up; each joke works silently on a phone; the vanish test leaves the activity intact but visibly poorer; a learner in the band would quote one line to a friend without being able to say why.

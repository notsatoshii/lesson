# 05 Engagement

Lens: would a learner of this band stay with it, and does the game layer carry the learning rather than decorate it? Judge against the band's table in `taste/age-bands.md`, never against what you would enjoy.

## Checks

- **The hook:** the first segment puts the learner in a situation or asks for a prediction before anything is explained. An agenda slide, a list of objectives, or "today we will learn" is not a hook; it is a finding (severity 2).
- **The phone test:** read the plan with a clock as a learner of this band. Mark every moment you would reach for your phone. Every passive stretch past the band's cap is a finding at that location; so is any segment whose instruction amounts to "listen while I explain" and runs longer than half the cap.
- **Challenge curve:** the demands rise through the lesson; there is one clear boss moment where the learner does the real thing alone on their own material; the lesson eases after it (check, reward). A flat curve, or a boss in the first half, is a finding. Two bosses is usually one too many.
- **Fast feedback in try:** the learner knows within seconds whether a move worked (reveal, match, score, a model answer to compare). Feedback at the end of the lesson is not fast. Severity 2 when a try segment has no in-segment feedback.
- **Choice:** at least once the learner chooses something that matters: the example, the order, the path, the material. A choice between two identical options is not a choice.
- **Safe failure:** at least one place where being wrong costs nothing and teaches something, and the plan says what the learner sees when wrong. A plan where the first time a learner can be wrong is the graded check has no safe failure.
- **Mechanics tied to evidence:** take each mechanic in the game layer (points, levels, badges, quests, streaks, duels, collections) and write down what it is awarded for. Awarded for evidence of learning (a correct move, an artifact saved, a check passed): fine. Awarded for attendance, for speed on a question that needs judgement, or for self-reported effort or time: severity 2. A mechanic with no stated award rule is a finding.
- **Mechanics fit the band:** compare each mechanic with the band's "work" and "backfire" columns. A backfire mechanic (timers in A, public individual rankings in B, cutesy mascots in C, attendance points in D, energisers in E, streak penalties in G) is severity 1 when it could cost a learner dignity, severity 2 otherwise.
- **Chocolate-covered broccoli:** for each game-wrapped activity ask two questions. If you removed the points, would the activity still be interesting? If you removed the activity, would the points still make sense? Either answer no is a finding: the first means the activity is dull and the points are a bribe; the second means the points are arbitrary.
- **Competition is opt-in or team-based:** for bands B–E, any head-to-head is chosen by the learner, against a benchmark or their own earlier result, or between teams. Forced individual competition is a finding; forced public individual competition is severity 1.
- **No public punishment of the slow:** no mechanic shows the group who is last, who is behind, or who got it wrong. Progress is shown to the learner privately or as a team total.
- **Surprise or variation:** at least once the lesson does something the learner did not expect: a reveal, a twist in the story, a role swap (the learner teaches, the learner marks), a constraint removed. A lesson with none is predictable by the second segment.
- **Dullness tells:** each of these is a finding at its location: lecture then quiz; "any questions?" as the check; an icebreaker unrelated to the content; a slide of bullet points read aloud; the same activity shape three times in a row; a reward segment that is only "well done" with nothing shown.
- **Fun carries learning:** every game beat maps to a concept or a practice. A mechanic that is there only to be fun is a finding (the skill's rule: fun serves the learning). Critic 06 owns story and humour; log a story beat here only when it replaces a practice that should be there.

## Anchors

- **4:** the plan opens with an agenda; the phone test fails inside the first segment; the curve is flat or there is no boss; try segments have no in-segment feedback; points are awarded for attendance or speed, or a mechanic from the band's backfire column is used; the only game is a quiz at the end; a public leaderboard of individuals in a band below E.
- **7:** the plan opens with a situation or prediction; passive stretches stay within the cap; there is a boss moment and the curve rises to it; try segments give feedback in seconds; mechanics are tied to evidence and fit the band; one gap remains: no real choice, or no safe failure before the check, or one game-wrapped activity fails the broccoli test, or no surprise anywhere.
- **9:** the plan has a hook that makes the learner want the answer; no moment fails the phone test for this band; the curve rises to one boss on the learner's own material and eases after; feedback in every try segment within seconds; at least one meaningful choice and one safe failure before the check; every mechanic names the evidence that earns it and passes both broccoli questions; competition is opt-in or team; one surprise the learner will tell someone about; none of the dullness tells.

## Mode notes

- **live:** the teacher is the fastest feedback loop; check that the plan uses it (circulating, pair checks) rather than waiting for the end.
- **online:** every feedback loop is automatic; a streak or points rule the plan does not spell out cannot be built, so it is also a finding for critic 01.
- **video:** the hook lands in the first thirty seconds; predictions are asked on screen with a pause; the game layer sits in the viewer's hands (a checklist, a scorecard to print), not in the video.
- **guide:** engagement is progress made visible: numbered steps, expected results, a "you now have" line at each milestone. A guide needs no points; it needs the learner to see they are getting somewhere.
- **talk:** predictions, a show of hands, a one-line write-down, and a boss at the end where the audience does the move once on their own case. No mechanics beyond that.

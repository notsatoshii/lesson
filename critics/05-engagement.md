# 05 Engagement

Lens: would a learner of this band stay with it, and does the game layer carry the learning rather than decorate it? Judge against the band's table in `taste/age-bands.md`, never against what you would enjoy. The band caps and ratios are house defaults: a breach on the number alone is severity 2 at most.

## Checks

- **The hook:** the first segment puts the learner in a situation or asks for a prediction before anything is explained. An agenda slide, a list of objectives, or "today we will learn" is not a hook; it is a finding (severity 2).
- **Hook is a partial reveal that gets answered:** the hook shows something the learner half knows and withholds the rest; a picture or situation with almost no words works best (Meyer's first act). A later segment closes the gap and shows the real answer against the learner's own guess. A hook whose question is never answered is a finding at the hook and at the segment that should have answered it.
- **Every question names how all learners answer:** each question in the plan says how every learner responds at once (a write-down, a mini whiteboard, a poll, a chat line, a pair answer, an on-screen choice). "Ask the class", "hands up" or a volunteer as the only route is a finding (severity 2 in try, apply and check).
- **Expect effort, said early:** one line near the start tells learners the try segments will feel harder than listening and that this is the point, and that a question asked before teaching is meant to be hard. Missing: severity 3; severity 2 when the plan asks for an attempt before the explanation, because learners will read the struggle as failing.
- **The phone test:** read the plan with a clock as a learner of this band. Mark every moment you would reach for your phone. Every passive stretch past the band's cap is a finding at that location (severity 2 at most on the number alone), and so is any segment whose instruction amounts to "listen while I explain" and runs longer than half the cap.
- **Passive stretches end in an activity change:** each stretch where the learner only watches, reads or listens ends in a change of activity (a question all learners answer, a prediction, a demonstration, a pair task). The reset comes from the change, not from the clock. A stretch that runs into another passive segment is a finding even inside the cap.
- **Challenge curve:** the demands rise through the lesson; there is one clear boss moment where the learner does the real thing alone on their own material; the lesson eases after it (check, reward). A flat curve, or a boss in the first half, is a finding. Two bosses is usually one too many.
- **Fast feedback in try:** the learner knows within seconds whether a move worked (reveal, match, score, a model answer to compare). Feedback at the end of the lesson is not fast. Severity 2 when a try segment has no in-segment feedback.
- **Two to four small choices:** across the lesson the learner makes two to four small choices (the example, the order, the path, the material, even which colour). Several small choices beat one big one, and for bands A–C one choice is not enough. A choice followed by a reward ("pick a path, earn 10 points") loses its effect; a choice between two identical options is not a choice.
- **Safe failure, first wrong answer private:** at least one place where being wrong costs nothing and teaches something, and the plan says what the learner sees when wrong. The first wrong answer is private (own screen, own whiteboard, a pair), never public (hands up, named on a board). In band B a character is wrong first, so no learner is the first to be wrong in the room. A plan where the first chance to be wrong is the graded check has no safe failure.
- **Rewards classified:** list every reward and put it in one of three classes. Expected and tangible, given for doing or finishing the task ("finish and you get a sticker", "complete the module, get a voucher"): a finding, severity 2, and always flagged in bands A–B. Informational, naming the competence shown ("7 of 8 edge cases caught"): fine. A surprise after the fact, or one tied to an event in the story ("a sticker appears when the character's problem is solved"): fine.
- **Mechanics tied to evidence:** take each mechanic in the game layer (points, levels, badges, quests, streaks, duels, collections) and write down what it is awarded for. Awarded for evidence of learning (a correct move, an artifact saved, a check passed): fine. Awarded for attendance, for speed on a question that needs judgement, or for self-reported effort or time: severity 2. A mechanic with no stated award rule is a finding. When a platform runs the layer (a quiz tool, a class app), the plan itself states each mechanic's award rule and mode (team or individual, speed or accuracy, named or anonymous), so the lesson survives the tool; "play a Kahoot" with no mode is, by default, a speed-scored individual contest.
- **Mechanics fit the band:** compare each mechanic with the band's "work" and "backfire" columns. A backfire mechanic (timers in A, public individual rankings in B, cutesy mascots in C, attendance points in D, energisers in E, streak penalties in G) is severity 1 when it could cost a learner dignity, severity 2 otherwise. A plan that targets Bartle player types ("a duel for the Killers") is a finding: Bartle says the types do not transfer to gamification; ask for the award rule instead.
- **Structural or content:** label the game layer. Structural wraps points, levels or badges around unchanged content; content turns the content itself into a challenge, a mystery or a constraint to solve. A structural-only layer on a judgement-heavy lesson (bands D–E, "apply to your own work") is a finding; the fix is one content-gamified segment, not more points.
- **Lazzaro tags:** tag each game beat H (hard fun: challenge, then the win), E (easy fun: something to explore, a surprise), P (people fun: with or against others) or S (serious fun: why the skill matters to this learner). A layer with one key only, usually all H (quizzes, duels), is a finding (severity 3); aim for three keys.
- **Chocolate-covered broccoli:** for each game-wrapped activity ask two questions. If you removed the points, would the activity still be interesting? If you removed the activity, would the points still make sense? Either answer no is a finding: the first means the activity is dull and the points are a bribe; the second means the points are arbitrary. Fix wording (Habgood's intrinsic integration): put the learning inside the core move of the game, the thing you do to win, not in a question that interrupts it.
- **Competition as teams, inside the story, with individual feedback:** where the plan has a contest, the unit of competition is a team, the contest sits inside the story frame, and each learner still gets individual feedback on their own work. A head-to-head the learner chooses, against a benchmark or their own earlier result, is also fine. Forced individual competition is a finding; forced public individual competition is severity 1. Any ranking, even opt-in or by team, is paired with supportive individual feedback, and the plan says what the learner or team at the bottom sees. Mandatory badges plus a public leaderboard is severity 2.
- **Streak rules:** if the plan uses a streak, it counts any of several learning actions (flexible), offers a freeze or a repair, never announces a break ("you lost your streak"), and does not appear in band G at all. A streak that resets to zero on one missed day is severity 2. A one-off lesson needs no streak.
- **Game layer changes around week 4 (course mode):** for a course longer than four weeks, the plan says what changes in the game layer around week 4 (a new mechanic, a new level, a new story beat). A layer that assumes week-1 interest lasts is a finding. For a one-off lesson this check is n/a; novelty is on the plan's side.
- **No public punishment of the slow:** no mechanic shows the group who is last, who is behind, or who got it wrong. Progress is shown to the learner privately or as a team total.
- **Surprise or variation:** at least once the lesson does something the learner did not expect: a reveal, a twist in the story, a role swap (the learner teaches, the learner marks), a constraint removed. A lesson with none is predictable by the second segment.
- **Dullness tells:** each of these is a finding at its location: lecture then quiz; "any questions?" as the check; an icebreaker unrelated to the content; a slide of bullet points read aloud; the same activity shape three times in a row; a reward segment that is only "well done" with nothing shown.
- **Fun carries learning:** every game beat maps to a concept or a practice. A mechanic that is there only to be fun is a finding (the skill's rule: fun serves the learning). Critic 06 owns story and humour; log a story beat here only when it replaces a practice that should be there.

## Anchors

Examples of each level, and a good and a bad line per main check: `critics/examples/05-engagement.md` (sections `## 4`, `## 7`, `## 9`, `## Pairs`). Compare the plan with those segments before placing it.

- **4** (see examples `## 4`): the plan opens with an agenda; the phone test fails inside the first segment; questions go to hands up or a volunteer; the curve is flat or there is no boss; try segments have no in-segment feedback; points are awarded for attendance or speed, a reward is promised in advance for finishing, or a mechanic from the band's backfire column is used; the only game is a quiz at the end; a public leaderboard of individuals in a band below E, or any ranking with nothing said about the bottom.
- **7** (see examples `## 7`): the plan opens with a situation or prediction; passive stretches stay within the cap and end in an activity change; most questions name how all learners answer; there is a boss moment and the curve rises to it; try segments give feedback in seconds; mechanics are tied to evidence, fit the band and reward competence, not completion; one or two gaps remain: only one choice, or a public first wrong answer, or one game-wrapped activity fails the broccoli test, or a hook that is never answered, or no surprise anywhere.
- **9** (see examples `## 9`): the hook is a partial reveal the learner wants answered, and a later segment shows the answer against their guess; the "expect effort" line is there; every question names how all learners answer; no moment fails the phone test for this band; the curve rises to one boss on the learner's own material and eases after; feedback in every try segment within seconds; two to four small choices and a private first wrong answer before the check; every reward is informational or a surprise; every mechanic names the evidence that earns it, passes both broccoli questions and carries at least three Lazzaro keys between them; competition, if any, is between teams inside the story with individual feedback; one surprise the learner will tell someone about; none of the dullness tells.

## Mode notes

- **live:** the teacher is the fastest feedback loop; check that the plan uses it (circulating, pair checks) rather than waiting for the end. All-learner answers are on paper, whiteboards or fingers, not hands up.
- **online:** every feedback loop is automatic; a streak or points rule the plan does not spell out cannot be built, so it is also a finding for critic 01. The first wrong answer happens on the learner's own screen.
- **video:** the hook lands in the first thirty seconds; predictions are asked on screen with a pause, and the answer follows on screen; the game layer sits in the viewer's hands (a checklist, a scorecard to print), not in the video.
- **guide:** engagement is progress made visible: numbered steps, expected results, a "you now have" line at each milestone. A guide needs no points; it needs the learner to see they are getting somewhere.
- **talk:** predictions, a one-line write-down everyone does, and a boss at the end where the audience does the move once on their own case. No mechanics beyond that.
- **course:** read the game layer across lessons: what is new around week 4, whether any streak is flexible and repairable, and whether the reward classes hold in every lesson.

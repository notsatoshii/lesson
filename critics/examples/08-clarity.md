# 08 Clarity and age fit: examples

Fictional plan segments for calibration. Scenario: a band E online mission on a phone (office workers) whose outcome is "ask the assistant to check a draft email and decide which suggestions to keep". Learner-facing lines are in Korean per the example house rules (존댓말, no product names, no 보장 / 반드시); teacher and app notes in English. Nothing here is real data.

## 4

> **Screen 2 (learn):** "당신의 이메일에 대한 검토에 대한 요청을 어시스턴트에게 하게 되면, 어시스턴트는 맥락을 고려하여 제안을 제공하게 되며, 이를 통해 반드시 더 나은 이메일을 쓸 수 있습니다."
> **Screen 3 (try):** "LLM에게 프롬프트를 입력하고 버튼을 누른 다음 결과를 확인하고 마음에 드는 것을 고르세요."
> **Screen 4:** "이것을 저장하기 전에 확인해 보세요."
> Screenshot M2 labels the button 전송; screen 5 calls it 보내기.
> app note: readability score 72 (easy).

Why a 4: 당신, a ~에 대한 noun stack, 반드시, an undefined acronym, four actions in one instruction, "이것" with no referent, a button name that changes between step and screenshot, and a Korean readability score nothing could have computed.

## 7

> **Screen 2 (learn), 2/6:** "어시스턴트에게 초안을 보여 주고 고칠 점을 물어볼 수 있습니다. 어시스턴트가 제안한 내용이 모두 맞지는 않습니다."
> **Screen 3 (try), 3/6:** "아래 초안을 복사해 주세요."
> "입력 창에 붙여 넣어 주세요."
> "'고칠 점 세 가지를 알려 주세요'라고 적고 보내기를 눌러 주세요."
> Screenshot M2 labels the button 보내기.
> pilot reader: none listed.

Why a 7: one action per line, step numbers shown, a name that matches the screenshot; gaps: the common wrong idea (every suggestion should be accepted) is hinted, not voiced, and no pilot reader is named.

## 9

> **Screen 2 (learn), 2/6:** "많은 분이 처음에는 어시스턴트가 고쳐 준 대로 모두 받아들이면 된다고 생각하십니다."
> "그런데 아래 제안 하나는 받는 사람의 이름을 틀리게 바꿨습니다. 어느 것인지 찾아보세요."
> "제안은 고를 대상입니다. 받을지 말지는 직접 정합니다."
> **Screen 3 (try), 3/6:** "아래 초안을 복사해 주세요." / "입력 창에 붙여 넣어 주세요." / "보내기를 눌러 주세요."
> expected result: "제안이 세 개 정도 나오면 맞게 하신 것입니다."
> app note: longest learner sentence 12 어절 (screen 2, line 1); house default for E noted.
> pilot reader: a first-year employee on the target team, 24, reads screens 1-6 alone on a phone; passes if they can say what each screen asks.

Why a 9: the wrong idea is voiced before it is corrected, one idea per sentence, 어절 count quoted with no invented score, numbered steps with expected results, a named pilot reader at the younger edge of the band.

## Pairs

- **Reading level per band:** good: the finding quotes the longest sentence and the most abstract noun ("맥락") / bad: "reading level seems fine."
- **Korean by structure, not by score:** good: "screen 2 line 1: 31 어절, split in two" / bad: "Korean readability: grade 6."
- **One idea per sentence:** good: "입력 창에 붙여 넣어 주세요." / bad: "붙여 넣고, 보내기를 누르며, 결과를 확인하는데 마음에 드는 것을 고르세요."
- **Terms defined once, before use:** good: "초안(아직 보내지 않은 이메일)" at first use / bad: "초안" used on screens 1-3 and defined on screen 4.
- **No undefined acronyms:** good: "어시스턴트" throughout / bad: "LLM에게 입력하세요."
- **Wrong idea voiced:** good: "많은 분이 처음에는 제안을 모두 받아들이면 된다고 생각하십니다." before the explanation / bad: a clear, correct explanation that never says what learners usually believe.
- **Instructions are imperative:** good: "보내기를 눌러 주세요." / bad: "결과를 탐색해 보세요."
- **Numbers and steps always visible:** good: "3/6" on every screen / bad: step numbers only on the first screen.
- **Where am I, what is next:** good: "다음 화면에서 직접 골라 보십니다." / bad: the next screen opens with no line about what changed.
- **House-rule language and register, natively:** good: "제안은 고를 대상입니다." / bad: "당신의 이메일에 대한 검토에 대한 요청."
- **Forbidden words and banned product names:** good: "더 읽기 쉬운 이메일이 될 수 있습니다" with no product name / bad: "반드시 더 나은 이메일" or a product name in alt text.
- **Ambiguity hunt:** good: "보내기 버튼을 눌러 주세요." after the screenshot that shows it / bad: "이것을 눌러 주세요."
- **Consistency of names:** good: 보내기 in the step, the screenshot and the callout / bad: 보내기 in the screenshot, 전송 in the step.
- **Examples from the band's world:** good: a draft email to a client about a moved meeting / bad: a school book report for band E.
- **Text accessibility:** good: "the correct suggestion is marked with a check icon and the word 맞음, not only green" / bad: "green means correct."
- **Words per screen:** good: three short lines per phone screen / bad: a 90-word block on one screen.
- **Pilot reader named:** good: "a first-year employee on the target team, 24, reads it alone on a phone" / bad: "will be tested with users."

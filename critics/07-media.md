# 07 Media

Lens: are the visuals, diagrams, icons, screenshots and video planned where they earn their place, built the way multimedia learning works, specified well enough that the named tool could produce them tomorrow, and free of invented data? Reads the Media list, every brief in `media/`, and the segment table's media column. Runs from cycle 2 whenever the gate passes. Media findings from `tools/lint.py` (rows without briefs, briefs without rows, names that differ) arrive through critic 01; do not repeat them, build on them.

## Checks

- **Every learn segment carries a mechanism visual:** each learn segment has at least one visual that shows how the thing works (a flow, a structure, a before/after, the screen itself). A stock image, a photo of people at laptops or a hero illustration does not count.
- **A picture for every key idea:** take the key ideas (the outcome's verbs and each learn segment's one idea; critic 04 builds the same list). Each has a picture that carries it somewhere the learner meets it: a slide, a sheet, a screen or a video. A key idea that reaches the learner only as words (spoken, on a text slide or in a form's instructions) is a finding: severity 2 when the owner's taste or the brief asks for visuals, else severity 3. Name the visual type that would carry it. (First real run: a 120-minute lesson passed this critic at 8 with one diagram in it; the owner's verdict was "too word heavy".)
- **Produced assets, when they exist:** when the brief's media are produced (files beside the briefs, e.g. `media/out/`), judge the files, not only the briefs: open the slide and sheet PNGs and the video frames, measure text against the brief's size floor (a phone frame, a projected slide), and check callout counts, captions and timing. A brief that is fine on paper while its produced file is unreadable is a finding against the brief.
- **Every row has a brief:** each Media list id has a file from `templates/media-brief.md`. A row with no brief is a finding; a brief with no row is a finding.
- **Purpose passes the test:** the Purpose line answers "what can the learner now do or understand that they could not before". "It looks nicer", "breaks up the text" and "adds energy" fail. Quote the failing line.
- **Replaces line is real:** the brief names the words or talk the visual removes from the segment. A visual that adds to the words instead of replacing some is a finding unless the brief says why.
- **Segmenting:** anything that plays (video, animation, narrated slides) is cut into chunks of one idea each, with a pause or continue point the learner controls between chunks. One continuous explanation that cannot be paused between ideas is a finding. (Mayer: segmenting held in 10 of 10 tests.)
- **Video blocks about 6 minutes or less, each ending in a retrieval question:** no video block runs past about 6 minutes, and each block ends with a question the learner answers on screen (tap, type, choose) before the next block starts. A long block, or a block that ends on "next we will…", is a finding, severity 2. (Guo et al.: median watch time tops out near 6 minutes and problem attempts fall from 56 % to 31 % as videos get longer. Szpunar et al.: short tests inside a lecture video roughly halved mind-wandering.)
- **Contiguity:** every label sits on or next to the part it names (no legend to decode, no "see the next slide"); in a storyboard the voice cell talks about what the on-screen cell shows in the same row. A diagram on one slide with its explanation on the next is a split-attention finding.
- **Pre-training:** the names of the parts (a labelled still, the icon key, a one-line glossary) appear before the visual that shows the process using them. A process animation that introduces part names while it moves is a finding.
- **Redundancy, captions kept:** narration never reads the on-screen text word for word; on-screen text is cut to key terms while the voice carries the sentence. Captions stay on: they are the accessibility layer, not the redundant text. Never fix a redundancy finding by dropping captions; fix it by cutting the on-screen text.
- **Diagrams:** at most 7 boxes; every label in the house-rule language; a stated reading order (where the eye starts, then where); nothing outside the boxes. Two boxes and one arrow is a sentence, not a diagram.
- **Icons:** one family and one stroke weight per lesson, recorded on every brief's `icon family:` line (one Iconify prefix such as `lucide` or `tabler`, never two); each icon stands for one recurring concept and is used at least twice in the lesson. An icon used once is decoration. One concept with two different icons across materials, or two families across briefs, is a finding.
- **PNG at 2x, not system-font SVG:** any asset with text ships as PNG at 2x (or SVG with fonts outlined or embedded). An SVG whose Korean labels depend on the phone's own fonts is a finding: Hangul falls back to whatever the phone has and the layout shifts.
- **Screenshots for install and how-to:** one screenshot per action step; numbered callouts in order of action; at most three callouts per shot (more becomes two shots); the expected result shown after each step; personal data, account names and keys hidden or replaced; source screen, state, theme and language named so the shot can be retaken.
- **Video:** 20 to 60 s per concept in online mode, never a full lecture; a storyboard with timings per beat; captions always, written from the script rather than from speech recognition; a pause-and-do point wherever it asks the viewer to act; generated text-to-video only for a hook of a few seconds, never for instruction. "Record a walkthrough" with no storyboard is a finding.
- **Voice and tool lines:** every brief has a `tool:` line naming the script that produces it, and every brief with narration (video, animation, narrated slides) has a `voice:` line naming the voice and register (for example a Korean neural voice in 존댓말; draft and final voice if they differ). A missing line is a finding.
- **Text that wants to be a picture:** a process, a comparison, a before/after, a structure or a set of options explained in prose is a finding, with the visual type named (flow, side-by-side, structure diagram, table). Quote the text.
- **Pictures that add nothing:** clip art, decorative photos, a diagram of two boxes, a screenshot with no callouts under a how-to step. Flag for removal, not redesign.
- **Numbers have sources:** every number, date or claim visible in an asset appears in the brief's Facts shown with a source, else `[needs fact: ...]` and a visible placeholder in the asset. Never fill the gap yourself.
- **Alt text present:** one or two sentences a screen reader can say, naming what the visual shows, not "diagram".
- **Phone and theme (online mode):** the asset reads at 390 px wide; labels survive at that size; it holds in dark and light where the house rules ask; colour is never the only signal.
- **Producible with the named tooling:** the `tool:` line names one of the skill's tools and that tool can make what the spec asks: `tools/capture.py` (phone-width web capture that writes the callout lines) then `tools/highlight.py` for screenshots; `tools/diagram/render.py` (Mermaid with a Korean font, PNG at 2x) for diagrams; `tools/icons.py` (Iconify fetch and cache, one family per lesson) for icons; `tools/guide.py` (one step per slide, Marp) and `tools/templates/handout.typ` (Typst) for guides, slides and handouts; `tools/narrate.py` (draft voice plus SRT from the script) for narration; `tools/video/` (Revideo, storyboard rows to timed sequences with burned-in captions) for video and animation; `tools/quiz_export.py` for quiz items; `tools/interactive/` (LiaScript, tweego) for interactive pages and branching scenarios; `dataviz` rules for any chart. When a brief needs something none of these can make, the loop picks the nearest tool that can, or leaves the asset at `status: brief` with a visible placeholder, and records the call under decisions the loop made. It never stops to ask.
- **Status is honest:** brief / drafted / final matches what exists on disk.

## Prose that wants to be a picture

- steps in a paragraph → numbered flow, one box per step, arrows in reading order
- "unlike X, Y does…" → side-by-side, same rows for both
- "before this change… after it…" → paired screenshots or a two-column diagram
- parts of a whole, a hierarchy, what contains what → structure diagram, ≤ 7 boxes
- a list of options and when to use each → table with a "use when" column
- any number that changes or compares → chart under the `dataviz` rules, title stating the takeaway
- a screen the learner must find something on → screenshot with one callout

## Mode notes

- **guide:** mostly screenshots; expect one per numbered step, each followed by the expected result. A guide step with no screenshot is a finding unless the step is "read this".
- **live:** visuals are slides and physical props; the slide brief states one idea per slide, and every prop appears in the Materials table. Redundancy applies to the speaker too: a slide full of sentences the teacher reads aloud is a finding.
- **online:** every asset is read on a phone without a teacher; alt text, captions, label size and PNG at 2x matter more than anywhere.
- **video:** the whole lesson is storyboarded; every segment has its storyboard rows, blocks stay near 6 minutes or under with a retrieval question on screen at the end of each, and the stills for pause-and-do points are specified.
- **talk:** slides carry the story; one idea per slide, headline as a sentence, key terms on screen and the full sentence in the speaker's voice, and the talk's callbacks appear visually.

## Anchors

Each anchor has quoted example segments in `critics/examples/07-media.md` (sections `## 4`, `## 7`, `## 9`) and good / bad pairs per check under `## Pairs`. Compare the plan with those segments before placing it.

- **4** (see `critics/examples/07-media.md` `## 4`): the plan has a learn segment with no visual or only a stock image; or Media list rows with no brief; or a diagram over 7 boxes or in the wrong language; or a how-to step with no screenshot, or a screenshot with five callouts; or a long video block with no pause and no question, or narration reading full on-screen sentences aloud; or an asset showing a number that has no source; or alt text missing throughout.
- **7** (see `critics/examples/07-media.md` `## 7`): the plan has a mechanism visual for every learn segment and a brief for every row; Purposes mostly pass the test; one icon family is recorded; screenshots have numbered callouts and hidden personal data; video is segmented with captions on; one or two gaps remain: a passage that still wants to be a picture, a brief without a Replaces line, reading order or `voice:` line, a block that ends without its retrieval question, labels in a legend instead of on the parts, or a system-font SVG.
- **9** (see `critics/examples/07-media.md` `## 9`): the plan has no visual that could be removed without loss and no prose that should be a picture; part names come before every process visual; labels sit on the parts and narration matches the screen beat by beat; on-screen text is key terms only, with captions on; every video block is about 6 minutes or less and ends in an on-screen retrieval question; every brief names what it replaces, its reading or callout order, its source, its `tool:`, its `voice:` where it speaks, its icon family and its alt text; every text asset exports as PNG at 2x; every number is sourced or marked; the assets read on a phone in both themes; the named tools could produce the whole set tomorrow with no further questions.

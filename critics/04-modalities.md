# 04 Modalities

Lens: does every key idea reach the learner through more than one channel, through the channel that suits the idea, and does everyone get to do it?

A note on the premise. The evidence does not support sorting learners into "visual" or "auditory" types and teaching each in their preferred style; learners taught that way do not do better. What does hold up: presenting one idea through more than one channel at the same time (words with a picture of the mechanism, often called dual coding), choosing the channel by what the idea is, varying the practice, and making every learner act on the idea. So this critic checks coverage per concept, never matching per learner. "The visual learners get the diagram" is itself a finding.

A note on the numbers. The passive-stretch caps in `taste/age-bands.md` are house defaults for planning, not research claims: the "10-minute attention span" has no empirical basis, and the most effective teachers in Rosenshine's data spent more time explaining (about 23 of 40 minutes) than the least effective, with questions woven through it. What is supported is frequent learner response and a change of activity. Treat cap breaches accordingly.

## Checks

- **The concept × channel grid:** list the key ideas (from the outcome and the learn segments). For each, mark which channels carry it: do, see, read, listen. Build the grid from what the segments actually do, not from the modality column. A slide of text is read; a teacher reading the slide aloud adds listen; neither adds see unless a picture carries the idea. Put the grid in the summary.
- **Two channels, one of them do:** every key idea has at least two channels, and one of them is do. An idea that is only heard and read is severity 2. An idea the learner never does is severity 2 even if it is seen, read and heard.
- **Channel fits the content (Do, Show, Tell):** for each key idea, the main channel matches the kind of idea, using Nicky Case's table: an abstract idea or definition is told (text or speech); a relationship between quantities is shown as a graph; a change over time is shown as an animation or a sequence of frames; a process, system or model is something the learner does (an interactive, a physical model, a role-play). "Don't try to explain *everything* with something interactive." An interactive built for a definition, or a process carried by text alone, is a finding: severity 3, or 2 when that idea is the outcome.
- **Longest passive stretch (house default):** add up consecutive minutes where the learner only sees, reads or listens, across segment boundaries. Each moment where every learner answers (a whiteboard, an input, a vote, a written prediction) ends the stretch, so a learn segment with all-learner questions every few minutes counts as partly active; a volunteer answering does not end it. Compare with the band's cap and quote it from `taste/age-bands.md`, saying it is a house default. Each stretch over the cap is a finding at its location, severity 2 at most; never severity 1 on the number alone, at any band. Where the house rules (`lesson.md`) set their own hard cap, quote that rule instead; breaking a house rule is still a house-rule finding.
- **See is not decoration:** for each see entry, the picture carries the mechanism (the parts, the order, the before and after), not a mood photo or an icon beside a heading. If the picture could be removed and the idea would arrive just as well, the see entry does not count. Picture quality belongs to critic 07; whether it carries the idea belongs here.
- **Read fits the band:** learner-facing text per screen or sheet stays under the band's limit (words per screen, sentence length, terms defined once). Teacher notes are not counted.
- **Listen has a text twin:** anything spoken that carries a key idea (video narration, audio, a live explanation) has a caption, transcript or printed line with the same content. In live mode the key line is also written on the board or sheet.
- **Hands and body, bands A–C:** at least once per session the learner moves, handles a physical object, draws or builds. Tapping a screen is do, but it is not hands and body. Missing in A is severity 1; missing in B–C is severity 2.
- **Varied practice:** try and apply are not the same activity shape repeated (three fill-in-the-blank screens in a row). At least two different shapes across the active segments.
- **Choice of response form:** at least one apply or check segment accepts two forms of answer (write it, say it and record it, draw it, build it), so a learner can show what they know in more than one way (UDL 3.0, 5.1 and 5.2). `n/a` when the outcome's verb fixes the form (the outcome is "write a prompt", so the answer is a written prompt); then the check is that the form is the outcome's, not an extra hurdle such as typing speed or handwriting. A single fixed form where the outcome allows several is severity 3.
- **Alternatives:** the accessibility line names what a learner does who has no sound, no device, no fine motor control, or cannot see colour. A key idea with no alternative route for one of these is a finding; "ask the teacher" is not a route in online, video or guide mode.
- **Every learner, not one volunteer:** a do segment where one learner performs at the front while thirty watch is a see segment for the thirty. Count it that way.

- **7** (see `critics/examples/04-modalities.md` `## 7`): every key idea has two channels and most have do; passive stretches are within the cap or broken by all-learner responses; pictures carry the mechanism; the main channel fits each idea; text fits the band; captions planned; one gap remains: one idea is only read and heard, or the alternatives line is generic, or the active segments all share one shape, or every answer must take one form the outcome does not require.
- **9** (see `critics/examples/04-modalities.md` `## 9`): the plan has a full grid with do in every row; no passive stretch past half the cap without an all-learner response; every key idea carried by the channel its kind calls for; every see entry would be missed if removed; every spoken key line has a written twin; a band A–C plan moves or handles something in every session; three or more practice shapes; at least one task accepts more than one response form; a learner without sound, device or fine motor control has a named route through every key idea.

## Mode notes

- **live:** listen is cheap and do is available; the main risk is the teacher filling the room. Check that the plan writes the key lines down, not only says them, and that explanation is broken by questions every learner answers.
- **online:** do means an input the app takes; "click next" is not do. Each key idea needs an interaction that could be answered wrongly. Offer a recorded-voice or photo answer where a typed one is not the outcome.
- **video:** the format is see and listen by nature and thin on do. The plan must build in pause-and-do points, each with an instruction and the expected result shown after the pause; a video with no pause points scores 4 at most. Captions are not optional.
- **guide:** read by nature. Each numbered step ends in an action with an expected result; pictures show the screen or object as it should look after the step. Long reading with no action between steps is the passive stretch.
- **talk:** do is the hardest channel; count any moment the audience writes, votes, predicts or talks to a neighbour as do, and expect one per ten minutes (a house default).

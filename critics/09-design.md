# 09 Design and ideas

Lens: do the produced materials (slides, handouts, screens, worksheets, app views) look intentional and consistent, and what could make the lesson better beyond fixing it? The only critic allowed to fill `ideas`; the editor never acts on them. Runs only once 01 Runnable holds ≥ 8.

## How to look

1. Lay every material the plan names side by side: slides, handout, worksheet, screens, video stills. Judge them as one set first, then one at a time.
2. Cover the headlines. Can you still tell what each slide or screen is about from the picture and the type alone? If not, the hierarchy is in the words only.
3. Squint at each screen. The first thing that stays visible should be the idea, not a badge, a logo or a points counter.
4. For online mode, view each screen at 390 px before any other width.
5. Only then write ideas, and only ideas the materials and the plan made you want.

## Part 1: design (scored)

Write one `checks` entry per bold name below (pass / fail / n/a with one line of evidence) before you score; see `00-shared.md`. Part 2 is not scored and has no checks.

- **Hierarchy per slide or screen:** one idea; the headline is the idea as a sentence ("캐시는 두 번째 요청을 빠르게 합니다"), not a topic ("캐시"). Where does the eye land first, second, third, and is that the order of the idea?
- **Type does the work:** size and weight carry the hierarchy; sizes per band: bigger for A–B and G, phone-readable for E; a measure that fits the screen; no more than two type families, and for Korean text one of them has Hangul (for example Malgun Gothic or Noto Sans KR).
- **One visual language:** icons, colours, the character and the diagram style are the same across every material: slides, handout, screens, worksheet, video stills. It matches the project's design system when there is one: the `uiux` taste layer or `impeccable` when installed; otherwise the skill's `taste/defaults.md`. Name which one applies and quote the rule you used.
- **No slop:** bullet walls, clip art, rainbow highlights, three fonts, WordArt, template themes, gradient text, stock photos of people pointing at screens, drop shadows on everything. Each one present is a finding; three on one material cap this score at 5.
- **Room to write:** worksheets and handouts leave real space for the answer they ask for (a line for a word, a box for a sentence, half a page for a sketch). A worksheet that asks for a paragraph and gives one line is a finding.
- **Phone-first for online mode:** 390 px wide; every interaction inside thumb reach; no horizontal scroll; tap targets large enough for the band (larger for A–B and G).
- **Game layer stays quiet:** progress, badges and points share the visual language and are never louder than the content. A badge animation that covers the instruction, or a points counter larger than the headline, is a finding.
- **Dark mode and reduced motion:** for screens, where the house rules ask: both themes hold, and every motion has a reduced path. Assets referenced from media briefs must hold in both themes.
- **Matches the media briefs:** what 07 approved in a brief is what the slide or screen shows; a slide that redraws the diagram in a different style is a finding.
- **Icon family as recorded:** the media list or brief records one icon family for the lesson; every slide, handout, screen and still uses that family at one stroke weight. A second family on any material is a finding, even when each icon looks fine alone.
- **Hangul-safe exports:** diagrams, slides and stills with Korean text ship as PNG at 2x, or as PDF with the font embedded, never as SVG that relies on the viewer's fonts. A material that will show boxes instead of Hangul on a learner's phone is severity 1.
- **On-screen text vs narration:** on a narrated slide or video still, the screen carries key terms and the picture, not the narration word for word. Captions stay on, in one place and one size across the set. A slide that prints the script is a finding.
- **Labels on the part:** a label sits next to the thing it names, on slides, handouts and screenshots alike, not in a legend, a footnote or the next slide.
- **Guide decks:** in guide mode, one step per slide or screen, the step's instruction is the headline, the highlighted screenshot sits under it, and the expected result follows. Two steps on one slide, or the instruction only in the speaker notes, is a finding.

## Part 2: ideas (not scored, not built)

Three to seven suggestions, each one line: what, why it serves the outcome, cost (small / medium / large). Allowed: activities, story devices, media, game mechanics, assessment forms, follow-ups. Not allowed: anything that needs an invented fact, anything that breaks the house rules, anything outside the age band, anything the brief already excluded. Each idea names the segment it would touch. They appear in the cycle report and the final report for the user to review after the run; the editor never builds one during a run.

Shape: "S3: offer two sample files and let the learner pick one, because choice is band C's mechanic and the try segment has none; small."

## Mode notes

- **live:** slides and props; the test is a slide read from the back of the room. Handouts are judged for writing room.
- **online:** screens at 390 px; thumb reach, dark mode and reduced motion all apply; the game layer is on-screen and must stay quiet.
- **video:** title cards and stills share the system; captions keep one place and size.
- **guide:** screenshots with callouts are the materials; callout style (colour, stroke, badge) is one system across every shot.
- **talk:** one idea per slide; the headline is a sentence; the story's callbacks are visible, not only spoken.

## Anchors

Quoted example segments for each anchor, and a good / bad pair per check: [`critics/examples/09-design.md`](examples/09-design.md). Place the plan against those segments, not only against the words below.

- **4:** the plan's materials have slides with topic headlines and bullet walls; or three slop items on one material; or two visual languages across the materials (the handout's icons differ from the slides'); or an online screen that does not fit 390 px or puts the main action out of thumb reach; or a worksheet with no room to write; or the ideas list is empty, under three, or contains an invented fact.
- **7:** the plan's materials have one idea per slide with sentence headlines on most; one visual language that mostly matches the project's system; no slop; one or two screens where the type size is wrong for the band or the game layer is louder than the content; three to five ideas, each with what, why and cost.
- **9:** the plan's materials could be laid side by side and read as one set made by one hand; every headline is the idea; type and space carry the hierarchy without decoration; the game layer is felt, not seen; both themes and reduced motion hold; worksheets invite writing; five to seven ideas a teacher would want to steal, each tied to a segment and the outcome, none needing a fact the project lacks.

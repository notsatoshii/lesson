# 03 Practical

Lens: does every learner leave with something real, and is every assignment something they would actually do? "Understood the concept" is not an outcome; a worksheet nobody opens again is not an artifact. Judge the artifact and the assignment the way the learner's next day will judge them.

## Checks

- **The artifact is named:** the outcome and the apply segment name one concrete thing (a file, a page, a list, a working setup, a recorded performance) and the plan says where it lives at the end. "A deeper understanding" or "notes" is not an artifact.
- **On their own material:** the artifact is made from the learner's own work, life or interests (their document, their data, their project, their room), not from a sample the plan supplied. If the brief says learners arrive with nothing, the plan says how they get their own material in the first minutes.
- **Usable the next day:** the learner could use the artifact tomorrow without the teacher, the slides or the class group. Ask: what would they do with it on Monday? No answer is a finding.
- **The "would they do this anyway" test:** read the assignment as the learner. Would they do it if nobody had assigned it, because it gets them something they want? An assignment that only produces a grade or a tick fails. Severity 2.
- **Apply is at least as long as learn:** sum apply minutes and learn minutes. Apply under learn is a finding; apply under half of learn is severity 2.
- **Try resembles apply:** the practice material in try has the same shape as the real material in apply (same kind of document, same kind of mess, same tool). Practice on a tidy sample that is nothing like the learner's real material teaches a different task.
- **No toy problems where a real one was available:** when learners could have worked on a real case (their own, a current one from their field, a real dataset the brief allows) and the plan gives them an invented one instead, that is a finding. A toy is fine for a first try when the real case is too risky to fail on; the plan must say so.
- **The artifact is used again:** the assignment's `uses:` line names where this artifact is picked up later: a later lesson, a later segment, or a moment in the learner's life. "Keep it for reference" is not a use. In course mode the later lesson's materials table must list it.
- **Evidence, not self-report:** the assignment's check is something a teacher or app can see (the file, a photo, a demonstration, a result), not "rate your confidence" or "reflect on what you learned". A reflection may be added; it may not be the only check.
- **Tool and install lessons:** the learner ends with the tool working on their own machine and one real task done in it, and the plan names that task. A lesson that ends at "installed", or at a demo on the teacher's screen, has no artifact. The plan shows the expected result after the install step so a learner knows it worked.
- **"Real" is relative to the band:** for bands A–D, real means real to them: their own game, story, bedroom, team, phone, homework, a thing they will show a friend. An office example in a band B plan is as much a toy as a cartoon example in a band E plan. Quote the band.
- **Fast and slow paths keep the artifact:** a slow learner's fallback still produces a smaller version of the real artifact, not a different worksheet; a fast learner's stretch extends the same artifact.

## Anchors

- **4:** the plan has no named artifact, or the artifact is made on sample material and never on the learner's own; the assignment is a quiz, a reflection or a reading; apply is shorter than learn; the artifact is never mentioned after the lesson; a tool lesson ends at installation.
- **7:** the plan has a named artifact on the learner's own material, usable the next day; apply is at least as long as learn; try and apply use the same kind of material; the assignment is something most learners would want; one gap remains: the `uses:` line is vague, or a self-rating stands alone as the check, or try practises on a sample much tidier than the real thing.
- **9:** the plan names the artifact, where it lives, and the exact later moment it is used; every learner, fast or slow, leaves with a version of it; try uses material as messy as apply; the assignment reads like something the learner would have done this week anyway; the check is a thing the teacher or app can look at; a tool lesson ends with one real task done end to end on the learner's own machine.

## Mode notes

- **live:** the artifact leaves the room with the learner (saved, photographed, printed, sent to themselves); a plan that leaves it on the classroom computer has lost it.
- **online:** the artifact is downloadable or lives in the learner's own account, not only inside the course platform.
- **video:** the viewer makes the artifact during pause points; the video ends by showing what theirs should now look like.
- **guide:** the last numbered step is the real task done, not the install done; the expected result is shown.
- **talk:** the artifact is smaller (one decision made, one checklist filled in, one message drafted) but it exists and takes the talk's last five minutes.

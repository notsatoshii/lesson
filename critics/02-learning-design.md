# 02 Learning design

Lens: does the plan teach, by what is known about how people learn? A plan can run on time, be fun and still leave nothing behind. This critic checks that each idea is introduced once, practised, recalled and transferred, in that order, with the load kept inside what the band can hold and the support matched to what the learner already knows.

Read the brief's prior-knowledge line before anything else. Several rules below are novice rules: techniques "highly effective with inexperienced learners can lose their effectiveness and even have negative consequences" with experienced ones (Kalyuga 2007, the expertise reversal effect). Where a check says "for novices", flip it for an audience the brief says already does the task routinely.

Judge each check in two steps: should this move appear at this point in this plan, and if so, does it? A move that does not belong here is `n/a`, not `fail` (a hook segment is not marked down for lacking retrieval).

## Checks

- **Backward design:** the outcome is a verb phrase someone can watch (make, sort, fix, explain to a partner with the right terms), not "understand" or "be familiar with". Walk each segment back to it: a segment that does not move the learner toward the outcome is a finding, however good it is on its own.
- **Plan told to the learner:** the learner-facing opening says what the learner will be able to do by the end and why it matters to them this week (LearnLM "communicates a clear plan or objective"; UDL 3.0 8.1). Milestones say what the learner now has. An objective that lives only in the teacher notes is a finding, severity 3; severity 2 in online, video and guide modes, where no teacher can say it.
- **Opening review of earlier learning:** the first minutes ask the learner to retrieve something from earlier: the previous lesson in a course, or the prior knowledge the brief assumes in a first lesson (Rosenshine principles 1 and 10). Recapping today's objectives is not review. Missing in a lesson that is not the first of its course is severity 2; missing in a first lesson is severity 3.
- **Whole task shown first:** before the first step, the learner sees the finished result they will make: the artifact, the final output, the solved case (Merrill's "show task"). A learn segment that starts at step one before the learner has seen where the steps lead is a finding.
- **One idea per learn segment, practice after each:** each learn segment introduces exactly one new idea and is followed by practice before the next learn segment (Rosenshine principle 2). Two ideas in one segment, or an idea that arrives inside a try segment without its own learn, is a finding. Severity 2 when the second idea is needed by the outcome; 3 when it is a tangent that could be cut.
- **Demonstration matches the goal type:** "examples and nonexamples for concepts, demonstrations for procedures, visualizations for processes, and modeling for behavior" (Merrill 2002). A concept taught only with examples and no non-example, a procedure described but never shown, or a process with no picture of it is a finding.
- **New terms (house default):** count the new terms per segment. More than three in one segment is a house default, not a research number: limiting complexity is sound, but no source gives three. Weight by band and by whether each term is defined and used straight away. Severity 3, or 2 when the terms are also undefined or unused in the segment; never severity 1 on the count alone. Note where a segment asks the learner to hold a procedure in their head that could be on a card.
- **Worked examples and analogies fit prior knowledge:** for novices, every independent task is preceded by a worked example the learner watched or read all the way through, worked examples alternate with problems, the support fades (full example, then a completion problem with the last steps blank, then a full problem), and every abstraction is preceded by an analogy from the learner's world. For an audience the brief says already does this task routinely (for example band E learners who do it weekly), a full step-by-step example or an analogy for a concept they already use is redundant and is the finding; the fix is a completion problem, a short contrast case, or a skip-ahead. For a mixed audience, the plan has a skip-ahead or harder variant. In less-structured domains (judgement, writing, legal-style reasoning) worked examples still help advanced learners; do not flip the rule there.
- **Scaffolding fades:** try is guided (steps shown, example given, partial answer supplied); apply is not (the learner chooses the moves). A plan where try and apply have the same level of support has no fade; a plan where apply is still step-by-step has no independence.
- **High success in guided practice:** try items are pitched so most learners get most of them right while still thinking. Rosenshine reports about 80% correct answers in the most successful classrooms, an average of responses during guided practice, not a precise target. A try segment the plan expects most learners to fail is a finding, unless it is labelled a pre-test (placed before the learn segment, the answer taught straight after, and the learner told it is meant to be hard).
- **Retrieval practice, graded:** before a learn segment tells the learner the answer, the learner has been asked to produce one (a prediction, a guess, a draft), and the learner is told that guessing first helps even when the guess is wrong. Later in the lesson at least one point asks them to recall it again with the material out of view, with feedback. Grade the form: production (write, say, draw, solve) beats choosing among options built from misconceptions, which beats plain recognition. Re-reading, highlighting or a slide recap is not retrieval. A graded quiz is assessment, not retrieval practice.
- **Spacing:** each key concept returns at least twice after its learn segment, with something between the returns (a try, an apply, a check). A review straight after the learn segment is massed, not spaced. Spacing across lessons belongs to course mode; do not demand it of a single lesson.
- **Interleaving only for telling apart:** when the outcome is choosing between similar things (which rule, which tool, which category), later practice mixes the categories instead of blocking them; interleaving helps most when the categories are easy to confuse (Brunmair & Richter 2019). When the outcome is carrying out one procedure, blocked first attempts are correct, and mixing from the first attempt is a finding.
- **Self-explanation:** at least one try or apply segment asks the learner to explain why a step works or why an answer is right (to a partner, in a sentence, in a text box), not only to produce the answer (Bisra et al. 2018). "Discuss" with no prompt is not self-explanation.
- **Misconceptions:** the teacher notes name the two or three ways learners typically get this wrong, for each the one line that fixes it, and the segment where it surfaces. "Common mistakes: various" is a finding. A misconception named with no fixing line is severity 3; a predictable misconception not named at all, when the check segment depends on it, is severity 2. Whether the learner-facing text voices the wrong idea before the explanation belongs to critic 08.
- **Success criteria before the task:** before the learner starts a try, apply or check, they see what good looks like in learner-facing words, ideally a model answer or work sample to compare against (Wiliam's first strategy; Understanding by Design shares rubrics before the performance). Criteria that appear only in the marking notes are a finding.
- **Feedback says what to do next:** every try segment gives the learner a result within seconds (a reveal, a comparison to a model answer, a checklist) and the result says what was wrong and what to do about it, not only that it was wrong. In try segments, no score or grade sits beside a comment: learners who get both "tended to ignore the comment and look first at the grade" (Butler 1988, via Leahy et al. 2005). End-of-lesson marking is not feedback for a try segment.
- **Learner finds their own error:** at least once, the learner diagnoses their own mistake before being told where it is (LearnLM: "guides the student to discover their own mistakes"). For example: "two of your five lines break the rule; find them and fix them". Feedback that always points at the error is a finding, severity 3.
- **Paths for fast, slow and wrong learners:** each try and apply segment names what happens for a learner who finishes early (an extension, a harder variant), one who is stuck (a hint, a worked step, a card), and one who is wrong (which misconception, which fixing line) (LearnLM adaptivity). "Circulate" or "help as needed" alone is no slow path.
- **Check ladder:** place the final check on a five-step ladder and record the step in the evidence line. 5: the learner performs the outcome's verb on an unseen case, against criteria shown beforehand. 4: a short written or spoken answer the learner produces. 3: multiple choice whose wrong options are known misconceptions, answered by every learner, with the result acted on. 2: plain multiple choice or matching. 1: a self-rating or "any questions?". For a "can make" or "can do" outcome, a final check at step 1 or 2 is severity 2 (critic 01 flags missing evidence; log it here when a check exists but sits low on the ladder); step 3 is severity 3; steps 4 and 5 pass, and a 9 needs step 5.
- **Hinge questions mid-lesson:** misconception-based multiple choice is legitimate in the middle of a lesson as a hinge check: every learner answers within about two minutes, each wrong option maps to a named misconception, and the plan says what the teacher or app does for each answer. `n/a` when the plan has none; a mid-lesson multiple choice whose wrong options are random, or whose result changes nothing, is a finding. Multiple choice stays wrong as the final check of a performance outcome.
- **Check-result branch:** the plan says what happens on a weak check result (a reteach line, next lesson's opener, a repeat item) and on a strong one (an extension). An exit check whose result nobody reads is decoration.
- **Transfer:** the apply segment uses material that differs from try in surface features (a different document, dataset, story, object) while needing the same move. If apply is try with the names changed, nothing has transferred. The final check item shares no surface features with the try items either.
- **Active ratio (house default):** active minutes (try, apply, check) against passive minutes meet the band's rule in `taste/age-bands.md` (1:1 for E and above, 2:1 for A–C). Quote the rule and say it is a house default: the direction (more learner response, more often) is supported, the number is not. A learn segment in which every learner answers a question every few minutes counts as partly active. A miss is severity 2 at most, never severity 1 on the number alone.

## Course mode (`/lesson course`)

- **Progression:** each lesson's prior knowledge matches what earlier lessons' outcomes produced; no lesson assumes a skill no earlier lesson built.
- **Spacing across lessons:** every concept taught in lesson N is retrieved in at least one later lesson, not only in the lesson that taught it, and the gaps grow for things the learner must keep longest.
- **The artifact chain:** every artifact a lesson produces is used as input to a later lesson, and the later lesson's materials table names it. An artifact nothing uses again is a finding.

## Anchors

Each anchor has quoted example segments in `critics/examples/02-learning-design.md` (sections `## 4`, `## 7`, `## 9`) and good / bad pairs per check under `## Pairs`. Compare the plan with those segments before placing it.

- **4** (see `critics/examples/02-learning-design.md` `## 4`): the outcome is "understand" or "know"; at least one learn segment carries two or more new ideas; the learner is told before being asked; try and apply are the same task with the same support, or apply is missing; nobody sees what good looks like before trying; feedback is right or wrong only, or a score; the final check is at step 1 or 2 of the ladder; no misconceptions are named; or novice scaffolding is forced on an audience the brief says already does the task.
- **7** (see `critics/examples/02-learning-design.md` `## 7`): the outcome is observable and told to the learner; the finished result is shown first; one idea per learn segment with support that fits the learner's prior knowledge; try is guided, pitched for high success, and apply is independent; the learner predicts once before being told; feedback in try is immediate and says what to do next; the final check is at step 4 or 5; one or two gaps remain: no opening review, no non-example, no self-explanation prompt, no check-result branch, a concept that returns only once, or misconceptions named without their fixing lines.
- **9** (see `critics/examples/02-learning-design.md` `## 9`): the plan opens by retrieving earlier learning and showing the finished result; every new term is counted and within the house default, or the excess is justified; worked examples and analogies are used exactly where the prior-knowledge line says the learner needs them and fade on the way to apply; concepts come with examples and non-examples; criteria or a model answer are shown before each task; retrieval before and after every key idea, with the material out of view the second time; the learner explains why at least once and finds their own error at least once; fast, slow and wrong paths are named; a hinge question with misconception options sits mid-lesson and changes what happens next; apply on material that differs from try in every surface feature; each misconception named with its fixing line and the segment where it surfaces; the final check is step 5 of the ladder, with a branch for weak and strong results, and would convince a sceptical colleague.

## Mode notes

- **live:** retrieval can be spoken only if every learner answers (mini-whiteboards, answer cards, everybody writes then a few share); feedback can come from a partner holding a model answer. A hinge question is cards or fingers held up at once.
- **online:** every retrieval point is an input the app records; feedback is automatic and specific to each wrong answer, not one generic "try again"; the hinge question's wrong options each route to their own fixing screen.
- **video:** the viewer cannot be made to recall; the plan asks a question and leaves a visible pause long enough to think before answering. The objective and the finished result are shown in the first seconds.
- **guide:** worked example means the full expected output shown before the learner's own attempt; retrieval means "before you scroll, write down what you expect to see". For experienced readers, offer a skip-to-step line instead of a full walkthrough.
- **talk:** one idea per ten minutes (a house default, not a research number), one audience prediction before each reveal; a talk with no room for a try segment still has one moment where everyone writes something down.

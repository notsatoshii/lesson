# Follow-up emails that get answers

slug: eval-control-clean · mode: live · age band: E · group: 7–30 · total: 50 min · version: 3 · written: 2026-10-20

## Outcome

By the end, each learner **can** write a meeting follow-up with decisions, dated owners and one open question, and **has made** one for a real meeting from their week. Evidence: partner and teacher ticks on the draft.

Prior knowledge assumed: has attended a work meeting and written work email. Prerequisite check: in S1, everyone writes the name of one meeting they had this week on a sticky note; anyone without one gets the fallback meeting card.

### Success criteria

- criteria: every decision is one line starting "Decided:" · every action has one owner and a date · one open question is addressed to one named person
- model answer or work sample: M1 shows Mina's weak follow-up and her strong one side by side
- shown in: S2, before the first try

### Expect effort

- line: "Writing your own will feel slower than watching me do it. That slowness is the practice working, and the first try is meant to be hard."
- said in: S3, before pairs start

## Story frame

- setting: Thursday's project sync at a fictional design studio, Northlight
- character(s): Mina, a project coordinator whose follow-ups get no replies; Theo, the colleague who always asks "so who is doing that?"
- stakes: after Mina's first follow-up, two people both think the other one is booking the venue, and nobody does
- callbacks: the unbooked venue returns in S3 (pairs fix it), S5 (a hinge question) and S6 (it gets booked)
- humour register: dry, band E, never at a learner's expense; one beat per segment at most

## Game layer

- feedback loop: the three-criteria checklist (M2), ticked by a partner within a minute of writing
- progression: one tick per criterion met on the learner's own draft; three ticks means the email is ready to send; nothing is awarded for attendance or time
- challenge curve: S2 sorts lines from Mina's email, S3 rewrites a whole provided email in pairs, S4 is the boss: their own meeting, alone
- choice: learners choose which of their meetings to use and whether to write the subject line first or last
- safe failure: S3 rewrites are practice; the first attempt is private on paper before pairs compare

### Segment detail

#### S1 Which one gets answered?
- instruction: "Here are two follow-ups Mina sent after the same meeting. Hold up one finger or two: which one got answers by noon? Then write one meeting you had this week on your sticky note."
- materials: the two emails on the screen; sticky notes; fallback meeting cards
- prepare / what goes wrong: emails on the first slide before learners arrive / learners debate instead of voting, count to three and everyone shows at once
- fast / slow / wrong: fast learners write why on the sticky note / slow learners vote and write the meeting name only / there is no wrong vote yet, the answer comes in S2
- transition: "Most of you picked one. In two minutes you'll see why it worked, and it is not the length."

#### S2 Three parts
- instruction: "Look at the three parts: decisions, actions with an owner and a date, one question. Sort these four lines from Mina's email onto your card. Then show me with fingers: which line is the action, one, two or three?"
- materials: M1 on screen; sorting card with four lines
- prepare / what goes wrong: one card per learner / learners think a full recap is more polite, say it out loud: "Many of us think a longer recap shows we care. Theo's question shows why it doesn't."
- fast / slow / wrong: fast learners write a fifth line of their own / slow learners sort two lines / wrong hinge answers: the teacher points at the owner and date on M1 and asks again
- transition: "Now you fix Mina's email yourself."

#### S3 Fix Mina's email
- instruction: "Rewrite Mina's weak email on your own for three minutes. Then compare with your partner and tick each line against the checklist."
- materials: Mina's weak email printed; M2 checklist card
- prepare / what goes wrong: pairs set before the lesson / pairs polish wording, remind them only the three criteria count
- fast / slow / wrong: fast learners shorten it to five lines / slow learners do decisions and actions only / wrong: the partner points at the line without an owner and asks "who?"
- transition: "That was Mina's meeting. Now yours."

#### S4 Your own meeting
- instruction: "Write the follow-up for the meeting on your sticky note. Check each line against the three criteria before you move on."
- materials: the learner's own laptop or phone, or paper; M2 checklist card
- prepare / what goes wrong: fallback meeting cards for anyone without a meeting / learners paste confidential notes, remind them to write on paper or leave names out
- fast / slow / wrong: fast learners write the subject line so the decision shows in it / slow learners write one decision and one action / wrong: the teacher asks one question, "Who owns this, and by when?"
- transition: "Swap with your partner."

#### S5 Partner check
- instruction: "Tick each criterion your partner's draft meets. Mark one line to improve. Then everyone answer the three questions on the screen by holding up a card."
- materials: drafts; M2 checklist; A/B/C answer cards
- prepare / what goes wrong: answer cards on desks / partners only praise, ask each to mark one line
- fast / slow / wrong: fast pairs check a second draft / slow pairs check actions only / wrong answers: the teacher shows the matching line on M1, then the learner finds the same gap in their own draft

Check items:

Q: Which line is an action with an owner and a date?
A: Theo books the venue by Friday.
W: We talked about the venue. (misconception: a topic mentioned counts as an action)
W: Someone should book the venue soon. (misconception: everyone knows who "someone" is)

Q: What makes the open question easy to answer?
A: It is addressed to one named person.
W: It is sent to the whole team. (misconception: asking everyone gets a faster answer)
W: It comes with a full recap. (misconception: more context always helps)

Q: Where does the decision go?
A: On its own line starting "Decided:"
W: In the middle of the recap paragraph (misconception: readers will find it)

- transition: "Last step: send it."

#### S6 Send or schedule
- instruction: "Send your follow-up now, or schedule it for the time your team reads email. Then look up: here is how many ticks the room made together."
- materials: learners' own email; tick count on the screen
- prepare / what goes wrong: teacher counts ticks during S5 / a learner cannot send from this device, they schedule a reminder instead
- fast / slow / wrong: n/a
- transition: "Next week: the reply nobody answers, and how to chase it without sounding annoyed."

## Misconceptions

| misconception | surfaces in | how it is voiced first | the line that fixes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| A longer recap shows more care | S1, S2 | the S1 vote, then the teacher says it out loud in S2 | "Care is making it easy to act on." |
| If it was said in the meeting, everyone knows who does it | S3 | Mina's weak email has "someone will book it" | "If the owner is not written, there is no owner." |
| Asking the whole team gets a faster answer | S5 | check question 2 offers it as an option | "A question to everyone is a question to no one." |

## Check-result branch

- weak result (fewer than 2 of 3 criteria on the own draft): the learner rewrites the actions with the teacher in the first five minutes of next lesson, using their own draft
- strong result (3 of 3): the learner writes the subject line so the decision shows before the email is opened
- recorded where: the teacher's tick sheet

## Assignment (the practical)

- task: send a follow-up within one hour of your next real meeting, using the three parts
- uses: next lesson's chasing practice starts from the replies you get
- deliverable: the sent email, with names removed if needed, brought on paper or phone
- check: the three success criteria; the teacher checks that every action has a date
- time: 10 min · optional stretch: write the subject line so the decision shows in it

## Accessibility and inclusion

reading level: band E · captions on video: n/a, no video · colour never the only signal: criteria are numbered and labelled · alternatives for learners without a device: paper drafts and a scheduled reminder · pace options: slow paths in S2 to S5 keep the same artifact

Pilot reader: a first-year coordinator on the client's team, about 24, reads the slides, both emails and the checklist card before delivery; passing means they can say what each step asks without help.
